How does data move between systems that never talk to each other?

They share a log. Producers append to the end of a tape, and each reader keeps its own place. Reading removes nothing, and most of what makes Kafka unusual follows from that.

One topic, one consumer group, one simulated clock. Each tick is a chance for every running consumer to process one record.

The model

Four rules explain most of it

Rule 1

A partition is an append-only log

Records are added at the end and get the next offset. Retention removes old records later, but consuming never does. Two groups can read the same data independently.

Rule 2

Order exists within a partition

There's no order across a multi-partition topic. If events about one customer must stay in sequence, give them a key so they share a partition.

Rule 3

One owner per partition, per group

That's what keeps a partition's order intact while it's processed. It also means more consumers than partitions adds spares, not throughput.

Rule 4

Progress is a number the consumer commits

The broker doesn't track each record as done. A group stores an offset per partition, and lag is the gap between that offset and the end of the log. Everything about redelivery follows from when that number gets written.

        When it breaks
        
Guarantees depend on what you built

      

      

        

          Commit after · at-least-once
          
Crash, and records come back

          
Experiment 4. The successor restarts from the last commit, so work between that commit and the crash runs again. Nothing is lost, and side effects may repeat.

        

        

          Commit before · at-most-once
          
Crash, and records vanish

          
Set “Commit: Before”, send 10 records, and crash a consumer while it’s working. “Never processed” climbs: the group has moved past records nobody handled.

        

        

          The practical answer
          
Assume delivery can repeat

          
Make handlers idempotent, for instance with a unique constraint on a record's ID. Turn on “Idempotent application” and duplicates arrive but do nothing.

        

      

      

        
Kafka's transactions give exactly-once processing for read-process-write flows inside Kafka. They don't extend to an arbitrary database or an email provider on the other side of your handler. That part is still yours to design.

        Fine print
        
What this model leaves out

      

      

        

          Brokers, replication and leaders
          
The tapes behave as one reliable log. Real partitions are replicated across brokers with a leader and followers, and acks and min.insync.replicas decide what “written” means.

        

        

          Partitioning and assignment
          
The default partitioner hashes keys with murmur2, and keyless records go in sticky batches, far larger than the two records used here. Assignment here is a simple range. Real strategies include cooperative-sticky, which avoids stopping every consumer during a rebalance.

        

        

          Commits and timing
          
Commits are offsets stored in an internal topic, and clients often commit on a timer. Lag here is measured from the committed offset, as the CLI does. A running consumer's real position is usually ahead of it. Sessions are measured in seconds, not ticks.

        

        

          Pausing a consumer
          
This models the client's pause(), which stops fetching while polling continues. A consumer that simply stops calling poll() for too long gets removed from the group, which looks more like the crash path.
